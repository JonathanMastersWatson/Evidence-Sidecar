# Settlement Layer

This folder defines the settlement layer within the
Cryptographic Verification Sidecar (CVS) specification.

The canonical CVS specification is defined by:

- `CVS_ARCHITECTURE_v2.7.md`
- `CVS_IMPLEMENTATION_v2.2.md`

If conflict exists, the canonical specification governs.

The settlement layer provides a public, independent receipt mechanism
for anchoring cryptographic commitments generated by CVS.

It functions as a notary, not a control plane.

---

## Purpose of the Settlement Layer

The settlement layer exists to:

- establish proof of existence at a point in time,
- anchor evidence beyond the control of the observing system,
- enable independent third-party verification,
- make tampering or retroactive alteration detectable.

It does not execute logic, store payload data,
or influence execution behavior.

---

## What the Settlement Layer Does Not Do

The settlement layer does not:

- execute application logic,
- validate correctness of evidence,
- interpret events or outcomes,
- enforce compliance,
- store sensitive payload data,
- participate in disclosure decisions.

Settlement operates downstream of observation.

---

## Architectural Role

Within CVS:

- Evidence is generated locally by the sidecar.
- Evidence is chained cryptographically.
- Evidence may be batched.
- Compact commitments may be anchored publicly.

Settlement is asynchronous and fail-open.

Execution MUST NOT depend on settlement availability.

---

## Ledger Requirements

Any ledger used for settlement MUST satisfy the requirements defined in:

- `LEDGER_REQUIREMENTS.md`

These requirements are property-based.

The architecture constrains behavior, not specific platforms.

---

## Cost and Batching

Settlement cost management and batching strategies are defined in:

- `COST_AND_BATCHING.md`

Batching ensures that settlement cost remains:

- predictable,
- bounded,
- decoupled from event frequency.

---

## Finality and Receipts

Settlement finality and receipt semantics are defined in:

- `FINALITY_AND_RECEIPTS.md`

Finality proves existence at a point in time,
not correctness or intent.

---

## Failure Considerations

Ledger unavailability, delay, or partition does not invalidate evidence.

Failure behavior is defined in:

- `07_FAILURE_MODES/LEDGER_UNAVAILABLE.md`

Deferred settlement MUST be observable.

---

## Non-Exclusivity

The settlement layer is intentionally replaceable.

Any ledger satisfying the defined requirements MAY be used without altering:

- Evidence Object structure,
- disclosure semantics,
- authority boundaries.

---

## Scope Limitation

The settlement layer:

- does not determine legal admissibility,
- does not enforce compliance,
- does not replace governance structures.

It provides timestamped anchoring only.

---

## Summary

The settlement layer witnesses that evidence existed.

It does not determine what evidence means.

If settlement is unavailable, execution continues — and evidence waits.
